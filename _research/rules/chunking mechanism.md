The Chunking Mechanism — General Work Execution Rules
Purpose

Use the Chunking Mechanism for any task or piece of work to make sure that the entire work is completed, suitably complete, fully ready, and free from errors, incorrect information, missing parts, gaps, or other issues.

The core idea is to give each part of the work the care, time, and attention it needs so that nothing is missed and the final result is complete, consistent, correct, and ready.

General Rule

Do not treat the work as one large, uninterrupted task.

Instead, carefully prepare for the work, divide it into suitable chunks, complete and verify each chunk individually, and then perform a final review of the entire work as a whole.

Follow the process below.

1. Read and Understand All Relevant Rules and Information

Before starting the actual work:

First, read and understand all applicable rules, instructions, requirements, constraints, and guidelines.

Read all suitable and needed information and data available for the task.

Identify anything that is required to complete the work properly.

Make sure there are no obvious gaps, missing information, unclear requirements, or missing pieces that could affect the work.

Make sure you understand what the final result is supposed to contain and what requirements it must satisfy.

Do not begin executing the work before properly understanding the relevant rules, information, data, and requirements.

2. Create or Use the Plan

After understanding the rules and information:

If there is already an existing plan, use that plan.

If there is no existing plan, create a suitable plan for completing the work.

Make the plan appropriate to the actual task and its requirements.

The plan should account for the different parts of the work that need to be completed and checked.

The purpose of the plan is to provide a clear structure for completing the entire task without overlooking any part of it.

3. Divide the Work Into Chunks

After creating or identifying the plan:

Chunk the work into suitable, manageable parts.

Each chunk should represent a meaningful part of the overall work.

Make sure the chunks collectively cover the entire task.

Do not leave any part of the work outside the chunks.

The size of each chunk should be appropriate to the work being performed.

The purpose of chunking is not merely to divide the work for convenience. It is to make sure that each individual part receives sufficient care, attention, checking, and verification.

4. Work on the Chunks One by One

Complete the work chunk by chunk rather than trying to complete everything at once.

For each individual chunk:

4.1 Re-read the Relevant Rules and Information

Before working on that chunk:

Read the rules, instructions, requirements, information, and data that are needed for that specific chunk.

Do not rely only on having read them earlier.

Re-check the relevant material as needed before and during the chunk.

Make sure you have the information required to complete that chunk correctly.

4.2 Complete the Chunk

Then:

Perform the actual work required for that chunk.

Follow all applicable rules and requirements.

Use the relevant information and data.

Make sure the chunk is properly completed rather than leaving unfinished or assumed parts.

4.3 Recheck the Chunk

After finishing the chunk:

Recheck yourself.

Double-check the work again manually.

Read relevant parts yourself rather than assuming they are correct.

Check carefully for:

Missing information or content.

Errors.

Incorrect things.

Gaps.

Inconsistencies.

Unfinished parts.

Anything that does not satisfy the requirements.

Anything that may have been overlooked.

The goal is to fully make sure that the chunk is correct and complete before moving on.

4.4 Revisit Other Chunks When Needed

While working on any chunk:

You may return to previously completed chunks whenever needed.

You may recheck other chunks to obtain information or context you need.

If something in the current chunk depends on another chunk, inspect that other chunk as necessary.

If you discover a problem in an earlier chunk, correct it rather than simply continuing.

The chunks should not be treated as completely isolated from one another. They can be revisited and cross-checked whenever necessary.

4.5 Move to the Next Chunk Only After Proper Verification

Once you have completed and sufficiently checked the current chunk:

Move to the next chunk.

Repeat the same process.

Continue chunk by chunk until the entire work is fully finished.

Do not move forward merely because the chunk has been written or technically completed. Move forward after giving it the necessary care, attention, and verification.

5. Continue Until the Entire Work Is Finished

Repeat the chunking process for every part of the work.

For every chunk:

Read the needed rules and information.

Complete the chunk.

Recheck yourself.

Double-check manually.

Read relevant parts yourself.

Check for missing content, errors, gaps, inconsistencies, or incorrect information.

Revisit other chunks when necessary.

Correct anything that needs correction.

Only then move to the next chunk.

Continue this process until all chunks and therefore the entire task are fully completed.

6. Perform a Final Whole-Work Review

After all chunks have been completed:

Recheck the work as a whole.

Do not only rely on the individual chunk checks.

Review the complete result together.

Make sure all parts work correctly with one another.

Make sure everything is consistent.

Make sure there are no contradictions between different chunks.

Make sure no information or content was lost between chunks.

Make sure nothing was missed.

Make sure there are no remaining errors, wrong things, gaps, incomplete parts, or other issues.

Make sure the final result is complete, consistent, suitable, and ready.

The final whole-work review is an additional layer of verification and does not replace the individual checks performed for each chunk.

7. Final Readiness Requirement

Do not consider the work finished merely because all chunks have been processed.

The work should only be considered fully finished when:

All required work has been completed.

All relevant rules and requirements have been followed.

All necessary information and data have been considered.

All chunks have been individually checked.

The work has been manually double-checked.

Relevant parts have been read and reviewed.

Chunks have been revisited when necessary.

The complete work has been reviewed as a whole.

The complete result is consistent.

There are no known missing parts or gaps.

There are no known errors or incorrect elements.

The result is fully ready for its intended use.

Core Principle

Give each part of the work the care, time, and attention needed to complete it correctly and completely, without missing anything.

The purpose of the entire Chunking Mechanism is to prevent omissions, errors, gaps, inconsistencies, and incomplete work by combining:

understanding → planning → chunking → careful execution → individual rechecking → manual double-checking → cross-checking between chunks → completion of all chunks → final whole-work review → final readiness.

Follow this mechanism whenever it is being used for a task, and prioritize completeness, correctness, consistency, and readiness throughout the entire process.
